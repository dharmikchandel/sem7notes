Unit 5.2 / Managing quality

QA prevents drift.
QC detects defects.

The central distinction in the notes is process emphasis for quality assurance and product emphasis for quality control. [pp.8–10]

QA / QUALITY ASSURANCE

Process-oriented

Planned and systematic approach to evaluate quality and maintain product standards, processes and procedures.

  • Main purpose: verify applicable procedures and standards are followed.
  • Uses auditing, reporting and quantitative measurements.
  • Monitors processes, evaluates products and performs audits.
  • Measures products/processes and audits SDLC steps and the end-product.
Notes pp.8–11
QC / QUALITY CONTROL

Product-oriented

Focuses on finding and removing defects against measurable specifications.

  • Main purpose: find and remove defects.
  • Controls variation from desired specifications.
  • Checks variation at each development step.
  • Uses reviews and manual/automated testing (V&V).
Notes p.8

6 ANSWER FRAMES
01. Explain McCall's quality factors.pp.19–21

Opening: McCall's software quality factors are organized into three categories.

  1. Product Operation (5): Correctness (requirements), efficiency (resources/code), integrity (unauthorized access), reliability (failure-free functioning), usability (ease of learning/operation).
  2. Product Revision (3): Maintainability (find/fix errors), flexibility (improve operating software), testability (verify requirements).
  3. Product Transition (3): Portability (platform transfer), reusability (reuse code in other applications), interoperability (system integration).

Diagram: Draw three branches labelled Operation 5, Revision 3 and Transition 3, then list their factors. [pp.19–21]

02. Differentiate QA and QC.pp.8–11

Start: QA focuses primarily on compliance with standards/processes; QC focuses on detecting and removing product defects.

  1. Orientation: QA largely process-related; QC basically product-related.
  2. Objective: QA verifies applicable procedures and standards; QC checks conformance to measurable specifications and controls variation.
  3. Activities: QA uses audits, monitoring, measurements and reporting; QC uses reviews and manual/automated testing (V&V).
  4. Management: QA informs management using quality data.

Nuance: QA can also include product evaluation and end-product audits. [pp.8–11]

03. Write on software quality metrics.pp.15–18

Definition: Software quality metrics are a subset of software metrics focused on product, process and project quality aspects.

  1. Product metrics: MTTF, defect density, customer problems, customer satisfaction.
  2. In-process metrics: Testing defect density, arrival pattern, DRE.
  3. Maintenance metrics: BMI, response time/responsiveness, delinquent fixes, fix quality.
  4. Formulas: DD = defects / product size; DRE = defects removed during month / problem arrivals during month × 100; BMI = problems closed during month / problem arrivals during month × 100.

Diagram: A tree splitting quality metrics into three branches, followed by the equations. All formula wording is from the slides. [pp.15–18]

04. Explain CMM and its levels.pp.30–31

Draw the staircase first: 1 Initial, 2 Repeatable, 3 Defined, 4 Managed, 5 Optimizing.

  1. Level 1 Initial: process capability left unspecified in the slide's table.
  2. Level 2 Repeatable: disciplined process.
  3. Level 3 Defined: standard consistent process.
  4. Level 4 Managed: predictable process.
  5. Level 5 Optimizing: continuously improving process.

Structure: Maturity levels → key process areas → common features → key practices. Explain that these respectively indicate process capability, achieve goals, address implementation/institutionalization, and describe infrastructure/activities. [pp.30–31]

05. Explain Six Sigma and DMAIC.pp.34–35

Definition: Motorola-developed quality model originating in manufacturing, later applied to service and software processes. Sigma is statistical standard deviation.

  1. State the Six Sigma concept: six standard deviations between process mean and nearest specification limit.
  2. State the stated target: fewer than 3.4 defects per million opportunities.
  3. Write DMAIC: Define opportunities → Measure performance → Analyse opportunity → Improve performance → Control performance.
  4. Its aim is ongoing improvement of existing business processes, with fewer defects and lower cost.

Diagram: Five adjacent stages, D–M–A–I–C. [pp.34–35]

06. Write notes on TQM and test maturity models.pp.32–38

TQM: Philosophical management strategy emphasizing an organization-wide quality culture, customer focus, facts, teamwork, senior management and continuous improvement.

  1. Four TQM elements: customer focus, process, human-side of quality, measurement and analysis.
  2. Test process maturity models: needed because CMM does not adequately address test-related issues.
  3. Purposes: measure/improve test process, inform management, locate bugs, reduce correction cost and detect critical bugs early.
  4. Models listed: Testing Improvement, TOM, TPI and TMM.

Do not invent: The available notes don't provide TMM maturity levels. [pp.32–38]

QUALITY & VIEWS

McCALL = 5–3–3

Operation: correctness, efficiency, integrity, reliability, usability. Revision: maintainability, flexibility, testability. Transition: portability, reusability, interoperability. [pp.19–21]

QA / QC / QM

QA → largely process, compliance, audits/reporting. QC → product, finding/removing defects, reviews/testing. QM → plan QA/QC, define/verify standards, execute/control. [pp.8–12]

COST TYPES

Prevention + appraisal + failure (internal/external). Internal before release at developer site; external at customer site in slide MCQs. [pp.6,39]

METRICS & EQUATIONS

DD = defects / product size. DRE = defects removed / problem arrivals × 100. BMI = problems closed / problem arrivals × 100. BMI > 100 → reduced backlog. [pp.16–18,41]

ISO 9126 = SIX

Functionality, reliability, usability, efficiency, maintainability, portability. Implementation: quality requirements definition → evaluation preparation → evaluation procedure. [pp.24–25]

CMM = FIVE LEVELS

1 Initial → 2 Repeatable → 3 Defined → 4 Managed → 5 Optimizing. Capabilities: 2 disciplined, 3 standard consistent, 4 predictable, 5 continuously improving. [p.31]

TQM = FOUR ELEMENTS

Customer focus, process, human-side of quality, measurement and analysis. Organization-wide quality culture and continual process improvement. [pp.32–33]

SIX SIGMA = DMAIC

Motorola; statistical standard deviation; <3.4 defects/million opportunities. Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control. [pp.34–35]

TEST MATURITY

CMM insufficient for testing-specific issues; models listed: Testing Improvement, TOM, TPI, TMM. No TMM levels provided. [pp.36–38]
