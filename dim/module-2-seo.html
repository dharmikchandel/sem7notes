    module-2 › seo › on-page-off-page

    On-page and off-page optimization

    Rank 1Asked 2022, 2023 and 202540 min
    The ideaOn-page is what you say about yourself, on your own pages. Off-page is what the rest of the web says about you. A search engine trusts the second more, because it is harder to fake. You need both: on-page makes you eligible, off-page makes you trusted.
    On-page SEOOff-page SEO
    MeaningOptimising the pages of your own website to rank better and earn organic trafficActivity elsewhere on the web that signals your site is trusted and popular
    ControlYou control all of itYou influence it; others decide
    Typical factorsContent, titles, headings, meta tags, URLs, images, internal links, speed, mobileBacklinks, social media marketing, brand mentions, reviews
    Tells the search engineWhat the page is about and that it is usefulThat others vouch for it
    Speed and riskFaster to implement, low riskSlower and relationship-based; spammy links can trigger penalties

    On-page ranking factors, in four bundles

    ContentRelevant, original, up to date, matches search intent; keywords placed naturally; shows expertise, authority and trust
    HTML tagsTitle tag, meta description, headings, image alt text, anchor text and link title
    Site structureClean URLs, internal links, sitemap.xml, robots.txt, no duplicate titles or content
    ExperienceMobile-friendly, fast, secure (HTTPS), easy to navigate

    Write the code for the KJSCE homepage (2022 Q2)

    The question asks you to implement any two on-page factors in code. Pick two you can write in five lines. The safest pair is the title tag and the meta description, because they are what appears in the search result.

    Try first (5 minutes): write the <head> of the KJSCE homepage with two on-page factors, then open this
    <!DOCTYPE html>
<html lang="en">
<head>
  <meta charset="UTF-8">
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">

  <!-- Factor 1: title tag. Unique, main keyword first, about 60 characters -->
  <title>KJSCE Mumbai | B.Tech and M.Tech Engineering Admissions</title>

  <!-- Factor 2: meta description. Persuasive, under about 160 characters -->
  <meta name="description" content="K. J. Somaiya School of Engineering, Vidyavihar, Mumbai: B.Tech and M.Tech programmes, admissions, placements and campus life.">
</head>
<body>
  <!-- Bonus factors: one H1 and descriptive alt text -->
  <h1>K. J. Somaiya School of Engineering, Mumbai</h1>
  <img src="campus.jpg" alt="K. J. Somaiya engineering campus building in Vidyavihar, Mumbai">
</body>
</html>

    Write one line per tag saying why: the title is the clickable headline in results and a primary relevance signal; the description does not rank directly but decides whether people click; one H1 states the page topic; alt text describes the image to search engines and screen readers.

    Off-page techniques

    • Link building. Getting other sites to link to yours. Three styles: natural editorial links (others link because the content is good), outreach and direct requests (you ask), and self-created links (directories and profiles). Quality and relevance beat quantity.
    • Social media marketing. Shares and engagement amplify content, bring traffic and lift branded searches.
    • Brand mentions. Being named on news sites, forums and review sites builds authority even without a link.
    • Guest blogging, influencer and PR work. Expert articles on other sites, collaborations and press coverage that earn mentions and links.

    The KJSCE examples (2023 Q3 option)

    The question wants four techniques and five examples about KJSCE. These are ideas you can propose; write them as what the college can do.

    TechniqueKJSCE example
    Link buildingAsk Somaiya Vidyavihar University pages, sister institutes and education directories to link to the KJSCE admissions and placements pages.
    Link buildingGet alumni and recruiting companies to link to KJSCE pages from their own placement and campus-hiring posts.
    Social media marketingPost placements, hackathon wins and technical festival highlights on LinkedIn, Instagram and YouTube, each linking back to the relevant page.
    Brand mentionsEarn news coverage of student achievements and encourage reviews on education portals so "KJSCE" is named across trusted sites.
    Guest bloggingFaculty and alumni write technical or career articles on external platforms with a link to the department page.

    Direct link requests and the categories of target (2023 Q2C)

    A direct link request is manual outreach: you contact a site owner and ask them to link to a specific page. The steps: find relevant sites, check quality (relevance, authority, traffic), find a contact, send a short personal message explaining why their readers benefit, and follow up politely. Do not spam or pay for links.

    The categories of potential links, with a KJSCE example each:

    1. People you already have a relationship with: partners, suppliers, customers, alumni, sister institutions.
    2. Related sites in your niche: engineering education portals, including sites that already link to similar colleges.
    3. Resource and links pages: pages that list useful resources, such as "top engineering colleges in Mumbai" lists.
    4. Directories, associations and listings: professional bodies and education directories.
    5. Educational and government sites: .edu, .ac and .gov style sites carry high authority.
    6. Bloggers, journalists and influencers: coverage, reviews and features.
    7. Broken-link and unlinked-mention opportunities: offer your page to replace a dead link; ask a site that mentions you to add the link.
    Not in your slidesThis categorisation is compiled from standard link-building practice (resource pages, directories, relationships, edu and gov, broken links). In the exam, write any four or five with an example each. Check your class notes for the textbook's exact list.
    How to write it for marksFor "explain on-page and off-page with example" (2025 Q3): two definitions, a comparison table, four on-page bundles, four off-page techniques, and one example site throughout (KJSCE or an e-commerce brand). Add a line that on-page and off-page work together.

    Meta tags and HTML tags

    Rank 1Asked 2022 (code) and 2023 (href)35 min
    The ideaMeta tags are information about a page that the visitor does not see but search engines read. They influence how engines understand your page and how it looks in results. Learn each tag as a pair: the syntax, and the one rule that goes with it.
    TagSyntaxThe rule
    Title<title>Page title</title>The clickable headline in results. About 60 to 65 characters, unique on every page, accurately describes the content.
    Meta description<meta name="description" content="...">The summary under the title. About 150 to 160 characters, compelling, unique per page.
    Meta keywords<meta name="keywords" content="a, b, c">Comma-separated, descriptive keywords. Add that Google today largely ignores it; your syllabus still lists it.
    Meta author<meta name="author" content="Name">States who wrote the page.
    Country and language<meta name="geo.region" content="IN">
    <meta name="language" content="en">    		Tells engines which region and language the page targets.
    Robots tag<meta name="robots" content="index, follow">Per-page instructions: noindex keeps it out of results, nofollow tells crawlers not to follow its links.
    Redirection tag<meta http-equiv="refresh" content="5;url=https://newsite.com/">Sends users to a new URL after 5 seconds. SEO tip: use a 301 (permanent) or 302 (temporary) redirect.
    Heading tags<h1> to <h6>One H1 stating the main topic, then H2 and H3 in order. Structure for readers and engines.
    Anchor text<a href="url">visible text</a>The clickable words. Descriptive, not "click here".
    Link title<a href="url" title="more detail">Extra context for a link, for instance anchor "Read more" with the title "Read more about the history of the company".
    Sitemapsitemap.xmlLists the important URLs so engines index them. Covered in the next topic.

    Optimising the <a href> tag (2023 Q2B)

    "Attributes and factors" means: everything on a link that you can tune.

    • href: a descriptive, short, working URL. Fix broken links. Use clean words, not page?id=482.
    • Anchor text: describes the target page, includes a relevant keyword naturally, varies across links. Avoid "click here" and avoid over-optimising the same exact phrase.
    • title attribute: adds useful context about the destination. Do not stuff keywords.
    • rel attribute: nofollow for untrusted links, sponsored for paid, ugc for user-generated. Pairs with noopener on external links.
    • target attribute: _blank opens in a new tab; use with rel="noopener noreferrer".
    • Placement and relevance: link from related content; internal links pass authority within your site.
    <!-- internal link: descriptive anchor, useful title -->
<a href="/admissions/btech-admission-process"
   title="Step-by-step B.Tech admission process at KJSCE">
   B.Tech admission process
</a>

<!-- external paid link: tell search engines it is sponsored -->
<a href="https://partner.example.com" target="_blank"
   rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer">Our placement partner</a>

    Headings, the cheapest win

    <h1>Ultimate SEO Guide for Beginners</h1>
  <h2>Understanding Meta Tags</h2>
    <h3>Meta Description</h3>
    How to write it for marksFor a "tags" answer, never write just the names. For each tag give the syntax, one purpose line and a KJSCE-flavoured example. That is the pattern the 2022 question rewarded.

    Check yourself

    module-2 › seo › robots-and-sitemap

    robots.txt and sitemap

    Rank 1Asked 2023 (Q1, 10 marks)30 min
    The ideaA robots.txt file is a plain-text note at the front door of your site telling crawlers where they may and may not go. A sitemap is the opposite: a list of pages you want found. One restricts, the other invites.

    General syntax

    # comment
User-agent: <bot name, or * for all bots>
Disallow: <path the bot must not crawl>
Allow: <path allowed inside a blocked area>
Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml
    LineMeaning
    User-agentStarts a group of rules for the named crawler. * means every crawler.
    Disallow: /path/Do not crawl URLs beginning with this path. Disallow: / blocks the whole site. Disallow: with nothing after it blocks nothing.
    AllowException inside a blocked area.
    SitemapFull URL of your sitemap.
    • Filename is exactly robots.txt, in lowercase, plain text. The question says "robot.txt"; write the correct name.
    • Rules are grouped by user-agent. A crawler obeys the one group that best matches its name and ignores the others.
    • Paths match by prefix and are case-sensitive.
    • It is a request, not security. Polite bots obey; it does not hide pages from people.

    Solve the 2023 question

    Allow Googlebot everywhere. Block msnbot from the whole site. Block every other robot from /tmp/. Block every other robot from any directory or file called /logs. State where the file lives and its URL.

    Think before you open it: how many groups do you need, and what does each bot do with the * group?

    Try first (5 minutes): write the file, then open this
    # Googlebot may crawl everything
User-agent: Googlebot
Disallow:

# msnbot is blocked from the whole site
User-agent: msnbot
Disallow: /

# every other robot: stay out of /tmp/ and anything starting with /logs
User-agent: *
Disallow: /tmp/
Disallow: /logs

    Why three groups: Googlebot and msnbot each get their own group, so they ignore the * group. That is exactly why "other than Googlebot" needs no extra work: Googlebot never reads the * rules. Disallow: /logs matches by prefix, so it blocks /logs, /logs/ and /logs.php.

    Location and URL: the file sits in the root directory of the web server (the top folder of the website), so crawlers find it at https://yourdomain/robots.txt, for example https://kjsce.somaiya.edu/robots.txt. It must not be in a subfolder.

    Honest note: prefix matching also blocks something like /logsheet.html. If the question insists on exactness, add Disallow: /logs/ and Disallow: /logs.php instead. Msnbot is Microsoft's older crawler name (now bingbot), but use the name given.

    Not in your slidesrobots.txt syntax is not in your PDFs but was a full question in 2023. What is above is standard and safe to write.

    Sitemap

    An XML file listing the important URLs of the site, with optional hints, so crawlers discover and index them.

    <?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<urlset xmlns="http://www.sitemaps.org/schemas/sitemap/0.9">
  <url>
    <loc>https://example.com/</loc>
    <lastmod>2026-10-01</lastmod>
    <changefreq>monthly</changefreq>
    <priority>1.0</priority>
  </url>
</urlset>

    Creating and using one: list the pages you want indexed (canonical URLs only); write them in XML as above (or generate with a CMS plugin or tool); save as sitemap.xml in the site root; mention it with a Sitemap: line in robots.txt; submit it in Google Search Console.

    Watch outDisallow: (empty) means allow everything. Disallow: / means block everything. One slash is the difference between a site that ranks and one that vanishes.

    Check yourself

    Where does robots.txt live?
    In the root directory of the website, reachable at yourdomain/robots.txt.
    Write the rule that blocks every bot from /private/.
    User-agent: * then Disallow: /private/.
    Does robots.txt hide a page from the public?
    No. It only asks well-behaved crawlers not to crawl it. Anyone with the URL can still visit.

    module-2 › seo › process

    The SEO process: research and (re)assessment

    Rank 1Asked 2022 (Q3)25 min
    The ideaSEO is not something you finish. It is a cycle. Research tells you where you stand and where to aim. (Re)assessment checks whether you got there, and sends you back to research when the answer is "not yet" or "the world changed".
    Memory hook: "Real People Implement, Monitor, Assess, Maintain" gives Research, Planning, Implementation, Monitoring, Assessment, Maintenance.
    PhaseWhat happens
    ResearchBusiness research (goals, audience, products), competitor analysis (who ranks, why), current state assessment (audit your own site: indexed pages, rankings, traffic, technical issues), keyword research (the terms to target). Output: a research summary that becomes your baseline.
    Planning and strategyTurn research into a plan: content, link-building, social media, search engine targeting, SEM and technical strategies.
    ImplementationDo the work: internal (on-page) optimisation and external (off-page) optimisation.
    MonitoringWatch spider activity, referrals, search rankings, website traffic and conversions.
    (Re)assessmentCompare what monitoring shows with the goals and baseline from research. Decide what worked, what did not, why, and what to change. Output: an assessment report with recommendations.
    MaintenanceKeep the gains: refresh content, earn new links, fix new issues, follow algorithm and competitor changes.

    The relation between research and (re)assessment

    • Research gives (re)assessment its yardstick. Without the baseline, goals and competitor picture from research, there is nothing to compare monitoring results against.
    • (Re)assessment gives research fresh evidence. What it finds (new keyword opportunities, competitors who moved, algorithm changes, goals missed) feeds the next round of research.
    • Together they make the large loop. The first phase and the judging phase are linked, which is why SEO is continuous and not a one-off project. The small loop, between (re)assessment and maintenance, handles routine tuning without restarting.
    Source of the phase modelYour slides do not list the phases. The six-phase model with a "(re)assessment" phase and the two loops is from the SEO Warrior textbook (O'Reilly), which matches the exact wording of the 2022 question. If your lecturer used another list, lead with theirs and keep this logic for the relation.
    How to write it for marks (10)One-line intro on the six phases (1 mark). Research with its four parts (4 marks). (Re)assessment with its purpose and output (3 marks). The relation, with the loop diagram (2 marks). The diagram is the quickest mark in the answer.

    Check yourself

    Name the six phases in order.
    Research, planning and strategy, implementation, monitoring, (re)assessment, maintenance.
    Four parts of the research phase?
    Business research, competitor analysis, current state assessment, keyword research.
    Which two phases are joined by the larger loop, and which pair by the smaller?
    Larger: (re)assessment and research. Smaller: (re)assessment and maintenance.

    module-2 › seo › content

    Content optimization

    Rank 1Asked 2025 (Q3 option)35 min
    The ideaContent optimisation means making content easy for search engines to find and worth a person's time. The tension: short-term content wins attention this week, long-term content builds authority for years. A sound plan uses both, and keeps neither cheap.

    What you optimise

    Search intentKeywords used naturallyQuality and relevanceTitles and meta descriptionsReadability and UXInternal and external linksMonitor and update

    Do not stuff keywords. Structure with headings, short paragraphs and bullets; use images and video; make it mobile-friendly and fast; track performance and refresh it.

    Short-term vs long-term content

    Short-term contentLong-term content
    AimQuick wins: traffic, visibility, sales nowSustainable organic traffic and authority
    ExamplesSeasonal promotions, limited offers, social posts, short blogs, PPC ads, optimised product descriptions, infographics, email campaignsEvergreen guides, how-tos, tutorials, in-depth long-form articles, video, case studies
    LifespanDays to weeksMonths to years; keeps ranking
    Cost patternOngoing spend, stops when you stopEffort up front, then very cost-effective
    StrengthFast results, targeted audience, data to guide strategyBuilds authority, backlinks and trust

    Content balance

    Quality over quantity; relevance to user intent; natural keywords; good user experience; technical SEO (titles, descriptions, alt text); promotion and distribution; a balance of on-page and off-page work; and continuous improvement from performance data.

    Content creation motives: build brand awareness and visibility; support the customer journey from awareness to purchase; boost inbound traffic; improve conversion rates; build authority on a topic.

    Duplicate content

    What: identical or near-identical content on several URLs. Why it hurts: search engines cannot tell which version to rank; backlinks get split (diluted link equity); visibility drops; repeated manipulation can invite penalties. How it happens: URL variations (www vs non-www, trailing slash), content syndication, printer-friendly pages, CMS configuration.

    Fixes: canonical tags to name the preferred URL; 301 redirects from duplicates; keep only canonical URLs in the sitemap; clean URL rewriting; configure the CMS; a clear syndication plan; internal links to canonical pages; regular audits (Google Search Console); write unique content for every page.

    Apply it: the clothing brand (2025 Q3 option)

    Imagine you are the digital marketing manager of an e-commerce clothing brand. Suggest and justify three long-term and three short-term content strategies for sustainable SEO growth.

    Try first (8 minutes): write three of each with a reason, then open this
    StrategyTypeWhy it works for this brand
    Evergreen guides: size and fit guides, fabric care, "how to style" pieces, with category pages built around real search intentLong-termMatches what shoppers search before buying, keeps ranking for years, builds topical authority
    Authority content: lookbooks, long-form trend and sustainability articles, designer or influencer collaborations earning backlinksLong-termBacklinks and brand mentions raise domain trust, which lifts every product page
    Content refresh and customer proof: update top pages seasonally, add reviews and customer photos, strengthen internal linksLong-termFresh, trusted pages hold rankings and convert better; signals the site is active
    Seasonal sale landing pages with optimised titles and keyword-rich product descriptions (for example festive season)Short-termCaptures peak-demand searches quickly, and the data shows which keywords convert
    Social media posts, reels and stories timed to new drops and offersShort-termFast traffic and engagement, feeds social signals back to the site
    Paid search or social ads, email campaigns and shareable infographics for limited-time offersShort-termImmediate visibility and sales while organic rankings are still building

    Link the two: short-term campaigns send traffic and data to the site; the best of that content is rebuilt as long-term evergreen pages. That is the sustainability argument the question wants.

    Check yourself

    Two examples each of short-term and long-term content?
    Short-term: seasonal sale page, PPC ad. Long-term: evergreen how-to guide, in-depth long-form article.
    Name four fixes for duplicate content.
    Canonical tags, 301 redirects, canonical-only sitemap, CMS configuration. Also unique content and audits.
    How can short-term content help long-term SEO?
    It brings traffic, brand awareness and engagement data, which shows what to turn into lasting content.

    module-2 › seo › keywords

    Keyword research, stemming, modifiers and process

    Rank 2Syllabus 2.3, not asked yet30 min
    The ideaA keyword is a phrase you want to rank for. Keyword research finds the phrases real people type, and works out which ones are worth targeting. The mistake to avoid is guessing.

    Four types, by what the searcher wants

    TypeSearcher wantsExample
    InformationalAnswers or general information; clue words like "what", "how"coffee calories; difference between cold brew and iced coffee
    NavigationalA particular site or page; they already know the brandYouTube; where is CCD located
    CommercialTo compare before buying; interest in a product or serviceiPad vs iPad Air; iPad review
    TransactionalTo complete an action or purchase; also called buyer keywords (buy, subscribe, sell)buy crypto online; sandwich places near me that delivers

    Word stemming and keyword modifiers

    Word stemming reduces words to a root, so search engines treat variations as related: consult, consulting, consultant, consultation. For SEO, write naturally using several forms of your keyword, because one page can then match many searches.

    Keyword modifiers are words added before or after a keyword to narrow or shape intent.

    Modifier kindExamples for "engineering college"
    Commercial or qualitybest, top, affordable, cheap
    Informationalhow to choose, what is, guide
    LocationMumbai, near me, Vidyavihar
    Time or action2026, admission, fees, apply

    Strategy and process

    BrainstormYour products, audience, competitors' terms, your own site
    ExpandKeyword tools (for example Google Keyword Planner), autocomplete, related searches, stems and modifiers
    EvaluateSearch volume, competition, relevance, intent
    Choose and mapPick primary and secondary keywords and assign each to a page
    Track and refineWatch rankings and traffic, repeat regularly

    Strategy points: very short, popular terms are highly competitive, so mix in longer, more specific phrases that convert; match each keyword to its intent; use it naturally in the title, headings and body, never stuffed. "Traditional approaches" means the manual way: brainstorming, competitor and own-site review, and basic keyword tools.

    Not in your PDFsYour notes cover keyword types in detail. Stemming, modifiers, the process steps and "traditional approaches" are the standard textbook treatment (SEO Warrior, chapter 11), given here at exam depth. Check your class notes for the exact steps.

    Check yourself

    Classify: "buy running shoes online", "Nike", "best running shoes review", "how to choose running shoes".
    Transactional, navigational, commercial, informational.
    Why does stemming matter for writing?
    Search engines link word variations, so natural use of several forms helps a page match more queries.
    Why target long phrases?
    Less competition and clearer intent, so visitors convert better.

    SEO benefits, challenges, SEO vs SEM, how search works, hats

    Rank 2Syllabus 2.125 min
    The ideaSEO is the practice of increasing both the quantity and quality of traffic to your site through unpaid ("organic") search results. Quality traffic means the right visitors who act; quantity means more of them.

    How search engines work

    CrawlSpiders scour the web and read the code and content of each URL they find
    IndexStore and organise what was found. A page in the index can appear for relevant queries
    RankOrder results from most to least relevant to the query

    Benefits and challenges

    BenefitsChallenges
    Most users click one of the top five results, so rank means trafficAlgorithms change often and can drop your rankings
    Improves user experience and usabilityStrong competition for popular keywords
    Top positions build trustResults take months, with no guaranteed position
    People who find you through search are more likely to share on social mediaTemptation of black-hat shortcuts, which risk penalties
    Organic traffic is unpaid, so cost-effective over timeOngoing effort: content, links, technical fixes, mobile and voice changes
    Partly from your slidesBenefits are from your "Why SEO is important" slide, plus cost-effectiveness. The challenges list is standard material; your slides do not give one.

    SEO vs SEM

    SEOSEM (paid search marketing)
    TrafficOrganic, unpaidPaid, through ads such as PPC on search engines
    Cost per clickNone for the click; costs are effort and toolsYou pay per click
    SpeedSlow; takes monthsFast; visible once the campaign is live
    Lasting effectLong-lastingStops when the budget stops
    Depends onAlgorithm and rankingsBid and quality score

    White hat, black hat, grey hat

    White hatBlack hatGrey hat
    Follows Google's guidelines. Slow but long-lasting. Quality content, HTML optimisation, restructuring the site.Violates webmaster guidelines (spamdexing). Spam links, keyword stuffing, cloaking, hidden text and links. Gains do not last.A mix of white and black hat techniques.

    Check yourself

    Crawl, index, rank: one line each.
    Crawl: find content. Index: store and organise it. Rank: order results by relevance.
    Give two black-hat techniques.
    Keyword stuffing and cloaking (also hidden text, spam links).
    Which one stops delivering the moment you stop paying?
    SEM (paid search). SEO results persist.

    module-2 › seo › tracking

    Tracking website activity and Google Analytics

    Rank 2Asked 2022 (Q2 option)15 min
    The ideaDo not start with the tool. Start with the goal, then ask which numbers would show you are getting there, then find where those numbers can be measured. That chain is also the answer to "how can one identify the parameters to measure".

    Ways to track activity

    • Google Analytics: a free service. You register, get a tracking code, add it to every page, and it sends visitor data to Google's servers for reports and a dashboard.
    • Google Search Console: search queries, impressions, clicks, indexing and crawl errors.
    • Server log files: record spider (crawler) activity and requests.
    • Goal and event tracking, and campaign tags (UTM): record button clicks, form submissions and which campaign sent a visitor.
    • Heatmaps and session recordings: show where people click and scroll.
    • Platform insights: social media, email and ad platform dashboards.

    How to identify the parameters

    GoalQuestionParameters to measure
    More visitorsWhere is traffic coming from?Sessions, traffic sources, organic keywords, new vs returning visitors
    Better engagementAre people interested?Bounce rate, average session duration, pages per visit, exit pages
    More enquiries or salesDo visitors act?Goal completions, conversion rate, revenue
    Reach the right audienceWho are they?Location, device, demographics
    Fix navigationWhere do people get stuck?Landing pages, exit pages, path through the site

    Your slides add: Google Analytics helps develop and optimise landing pages (how relevant were they?), finds navigation problems, and improves AdWords performance by keyword, timing and geography. A keyword with a high average time on site probably brought more valuable visitors. Analytics compares many things within your site but does not directly compare you with other sites.

    SEO process

    Research, Planning, Implementation, Monitoring, (Re)assessment, Maintenance. Large loop: (re)assessment back to research. Small loop: (re)assessment and maintenance.

    Research has: business, competitors, current state, keywords.

    Search engines: crawl, index, rank.

    Hats: white (guidelines, slow, lasting), black (spam, cloaking, stuffing), grey (mix).

    On-page vs off-page

    On-page (you control): content, title, description, headings, alt text, URLs, internal links, speed, mobile.

    Off-page (others vouch): link building, social media marketing, brand mentions, guest posts, PR.

    Direct link request targets: relationships, related sites, resource pages, directories, edu and gov, bloggers and press, broken links.

    Keywords

    Four types: informational (coffee calories), navigational (YouTube), commercial (iPad review), transactional (buy crypto online).

    Stemming: consult, consulting, consultant. Modifiers: best, cheap, near me, 2026.

    Process: brainstorm, expand, evaluate, choose and map, track.

    Content

    Short-term: sale page, PPC, social post, short blog, infographic. Long-term: evergreen guides, long-form, video, refreshes.

    Duplicate content fixes: canonical, 301, canonical-only sitemap, CMS setup, unique content, audits.

    Sitemap: sitemap.xml in root, listed in robots.txt, submitted in Search Console.
