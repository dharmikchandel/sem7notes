4.1 / Fundamentals

Define SMM, explain its trade-offs

Social media marketing (SMM) uses social platforms to promote a business, interact with its audience and achieve goals such as awareness, traffic, leads, sales or loyalty. Social media are sites and apps for creating, exchanging and discussing content. SMM activities include content creation, scheduling, analytics, listening, community management and advertising.

  1. Fan acquisition
  2. Engagement
  3. Amplification
  4. Community
  5. Brand strength / sales

Acquire fans, encourage interactions, amplify through sharing, build a community and connect brand strength to outcomes.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Awareness and large targeted reach through shareable posts and ads.Time commitment to create, publish, monitor and respond consistently.
Inbound traffic, leads and sales through clear calls to action.Negative feedback is publicly visible and can spread quickly.
Faster two-way communication, updates and customer support.Qualified people and moderation cost money.
Engagement, loyalty and community through continued interaction.Competitors can observe and imitate campaign tactics.
Cost-effective testing and audience insight from clicks and interactions.Platform dependence and noisy metrics can complicate measurement.
Search discovery of public content and possible referral or citation opportunities.High visibility brings reputational risk when claims or responses are mishandled.
PlatformWhy you would choose it
FacebookPages, groups, targeted promotions and customer communication.
X / TwitterShort timely updates, public feedback and support.
InstagramVisual branding, stories, videos and product demonstrations.
LinkedInProfessional communities, B2B credibility, recruitment and leads.
PinterestVisual discovery through pins and boards that can drive referral visits.
YouTubeSearchable demonstrations, tutorials and branded videos.
SnapchatEvent stories, filters and short-lived visual messages.
WhatsAppOpt-in updates, service, catalogs and direct conversations.
Other slide examplesShort-form video on TikTok, interest discussion on Reddit, question-led discovery on Quora and audio conversation on Clubhouse.

Digital versus traditional. Digital channels include websites, social, search and video and allow direct interaction, targeting, tracking and rapid testing. Traditional channels include print, mail, outdoor, TV and radio and can deliver broad or local exposure through established formats. In a comparison answer, use channel, targeting, feedback, measurement and flexibility.

Use platform functions and audience fit when giving examples. The platform rankings and user counts in the early slides are dated snapshots.

4.1 / Strategy

Build a strategic marketing plan

  1. Set goals and objectives. Choose awareness, leads, traffic, sales, engagement, community, support, press mentions or listening. Give the main objective a target and date.
  2. Research the audience. Establish who they are, what interests them, where they are active and what formats they consume.
  3. Research competitors. Compare channel choice, formats, frequency, tone, hashtags and response style.
  4. Select platforms. Match audience behavior and formats to the business goal.
  5. Set brand guidelines. Record voice, emojis, hashtags, response protocol and prohibited topics.
  6. Build the initial presence. Add business details, contacts, site link, recognizable imagery, a launch post and relevant connections.
  7. Choose content. Plan photos, graphics, videos, articles, livestreams and Stories around audience needs and campaign goals.
  8. Create a calendar. Assign a platform, date, format, owner, approval and CTA to each post.
  9. Run promotions. Use relevant offers such as storewide discounts, launches, new-customer deals or event and webinar promotions.
  10. Test and evaluate. Compare followers, reach, engagement, clicks and conversions against the objective. Adjust targeting, timing, creative or spend.
  1. Goal
  2. Audience and competitor research
  3. Channel and content
  4. Publish and promote
  5. Measure and improve

4.1 / Research

Segment B2C and research B2B

B2C segmentation divides individual consumers into groups with shared characteristics or needs. It helps tailor channel, message, format and timing, improving relevance, engagement and return on spend.

SegmentBasisExam example
DemographicAge, income, occupation, education, gender or household.Affordable plans for university students.
GeographicCountry, city, postal code, neighborhood or climate.A delivery ad targets addresses the store serves.
Life-stage / behavioralLife events, purchase habits, use rate and loyalty.New parents see a newborn essentials bundle. Loyal customers see a repeat-order offer.
PsychographicActivities, interests, opinions, values and lifestyle.An eco-conscious audience sees a reusable packaging message.
Affinity / interest groupShared hobbies, passions and communities.A sporting-goods brand promotes a beginner kit among cycling enthusiasts.

B2B market research studies how organizations buy from other organizations. Purchases are typically higher-value and slower, with several stakeholders such as users, managers, procurement and approvers. Identify the decision-makers, their criteria, their buying process and where they gather online.

  1. Segment by industry, size, location and business need. Identify roles, authority, pain points and approval steps.
  2. Research company pages, relevant LinkedIn groups, professional networks and directories. Use audience and trend reports to check channel fit. The notes mention NFP, SitePoint, Quantcast, Alexa and Google Trends as examples or legacy references.
  3. Check influencers, decision-maker conversations and competitors. Record what their audiences engage with.
  4. Publish case studies, research, guides or webinars demonstrating expertise. Use social media for awareness, thought leadership, problem-solving, support and loyalty. Track qualified leads, meetings and pipeline.

Other online research. State what you need to learn, time-box the work and identify influential sites and users. Use engagement and relevance to judge influence. Study audience motivation (information, entertainment, community or self-promotion), validate competitor patterns and run a small pilot. Monitor clicks, comments, shares and responses. Brandwatch and Ragan are examples for discovering conversations or influencers. The notes also cite Twitaholic as a historical example. SimilarSites helps find competitors, Google Trends helps identify search interest, and Social Media Examiner, HubSpot or MDG reports can inform the plan.

4.2 / Cybersocial campaign

Run a campaign and protect the brand

A cybersocial campaign is a coordinated social effort serving a specific business goal on one or more platforms, with focused targeting and measurable results.

  1. Objective and audience
  2. Schedule and roles
  3. Policy and rights
  4. Publish and respond
  5. Measure and adjust

Manage the schedule

  1. Bound time commitment. Select activity days and time blocks for creation, publishing and replies. Allow ramp-up time to learn tools, practise responding and pilot the content.
  2. Develop a social date book. Use a spreadsheet or project calendar with day, week and month views, chronological ordering and recurring tasks. Record date/time, channel, content, CTA, owner and approval state.
  3. Create a dashboard. Use Hootsuite or a similar dashboard to schedule and distribute posts, watch mentions, track interactions and measure outcomes across channels.

Write a social media policy

  • Accountability and disclosure. Assign authors and approvers, say who you represent and disclose relevant commercial relationships.
  • Tone and conduct. Be respectful, follow community norms, consider customers, competitors and potential recruits, and escalate complaints or errors.
  • Confidentiality and rights. Protect client data, trade secrets and NDA material. Respect copyright, trademarks and permission conditions.
  • Work boundaries. Define authorized spokespeople, access and use of personal social media during work.

Stay on the right side of the law

  1. Obtain permission. Use original assets, licensed stock, properly licensed Creative Commons material or a verified public-domain source. Record the asset, how it will be used, requested credit and written permission. A downloadable image still needs a rights check.
  2. Reveal endorsement relationships. Disclose sponsorships, payments, gifts and free products clearly in the relevant post or review. Use honest claims and follow applicable promotion and advertising rules.
  3. Protect data and confidentiality. Obtain appropriate permission for customer content or communications and avoid publishing private or confidential details.

Protect the brand. Copyright covers original creative work in fixed form, such as text, images, audio or video. A notice can identify the owner, e.g. © 2026 Example Co. Trademarks and service marks identify the commercial source of goods and services by name or logo. Reserve short, consistent handles to reduce brandjacking, monitor brand mentions and impersonation, and use an escalation process for complaints.

4.3 / Tools

Update discovery, shorten links and sell

Notify search engines of updates. For your own website or blog, publish the changed page, keep the XML sitemap current and submit it through the search-engine webmaster tools. An important URL can be submitted for recrawl via URL Inspection where supported. RSS can notify subscribers and distribution tools about new posts. Public social posts rely on each platform’s discovery and search-engine crawl/indexing access. Submission supports discovery and recrawl. Indexing and position depend on the search engine.

Snipping ugly URLs replaces a long destination address with a compact redirect. It makes links cleaner, easier to copy across channels and easier to fit in constrained posts. Branded URLs can aid recognition. Click counts, referrers or geography can help compare channels, and an underlying tagged destination can support conversion measurement.

Tools. Bitly supports shortening, branded links and reporting. TinyURL is a simple shortener. Ow.ly is the Hootsuite example. Rebrandly emphasizes custom domains. The slides list goo.gl as a historical example.

Example. Put campaign tags on a product URL, create a branded short link and share it in the post. Check clicks in the shortening tool and sales from tagged traffic in analytics.

Social e-commerce

Connect discovery to a product listing and purchase path through catalogs, storefronts, tags or shopping links. In the notes, Facebook Shops and Instagram Shopping illustrate catalogs and product tags, Pinterest Buyable Pins illustrate product discovery, and Twitter Cards illustrate product previews that link to purchase pages. Shopify, BigCommerce and Ecwid illustrate integrations for listings, inventory and orders.

  1. Discover product
  2. Open product details
  3. Checkout
  4. Track sales and feedback

Benefits include buying convenience, product exposure, immediate feedback, measurable sales and synced inventory. Example: tag items shown in a demo, route buyers to an available checkout, and compare orders by post.

4.4 / Search

Optimize social media and gain live visibility

Social media optimization (SMO) improves the discoverability and shareability of profiles and content. It serves internal discovery through platform search and external discovery through indexable profiles, posts and media in web search. Benefits include awareness, relevant traffic and more opportunities for citations and engagement.

  1. Search terms. Use a consistent set of relevant phrases in the profile name or bio, titles, first paragraphs, URLs and descriptions. Write naturally. The slides suggest 3–5 focus terms and 2–6% density as a classroom heuristic. Lead an exam answer with relevance and readability.
  2. Profiles and platforms. Complete public profiles and link them to the website where relevant. Add industry terms to LinkedIn company descriptions, relevant hashtags to X/Twitter, a descriptive Facebook page, and keyworded Pinterest boards and pins. Google+ is a historical slide example.
  3. Blogs. Use a brand-controlled domain, meaningful headings and URLs, fresh useful content and reputable inbound links.
  4. Media. Use descriptive filenames, image alt text, accurate captions, transcripts and metadata. Publish searchable videos and podcasts with useful descriptions and a destination link.
  5. Mobile and local. Use responsive, fast pages and local terms such as city, neighborhood and service area.
  6. Review impact. Compare search discovery, reach, engagement, clicks and referral traffic, then improve weak content.

Real-time search surfaces recent public posts and updates about live topics. Visibility depends on recency, relevance, activity, privacy and whether a platform exposes content to external indexing.

  1. Find rising terms with Google Trends and platform search. Listen for relevant live conversations and questions.
  2. Publish timely useful updates from a public profile. Use relevant keywords and event or location hashtags.
  3. Reply and engage during the event. Schedule routine posts in advance and reserve time to respond live.
  4. Check public results in native social search and relevant external search tools. The notes list services such as IceRocket and Google+ search as historical examples. Track discovery and referral traffic as well as engagement.

Example. A college posts public event updates with a consistent event hashtag, captions demo videos, answers questions live and measures hashtag discovery, registration clicks and sign-ups.

Design a Facebook marketing plan for AtUrDoorStepMay–Jun 2021 Q4 alternative · 10 marks

AtUrDoorStep delivers daily essentials within 24 hours in its service area. Goal: build trust and acquire first orders. Example targets for 30 days: reach 20,000 eligible residents, generate 1,000 link clicks and obtain 100 first orders. With ₹10,000 spend and 100 attributed orders, CPA is ₹100.

  1. Target. Focus on serviceable neighborhoods, busy households, students and working professionals. Split prospecting and eligible warm audiences.
  2. Page and promise. Set up a branded Page, service hours, delivery coverage, order link, delivery terms and pinned FAQ. Use a clear “Order now” CTA.
  3. Content and schedule. Show a 24-hour delivery demo on Monday, a basket or customer story on Wednesday and a weekend essentials offer on Friday. Assign daily response coverage.
  4. Paid and organic. Combine useful organic posts, local community engagement and geo-targeted ads. Test convenience versus a first-order incentive with comparable budgets. Route to a mobile order page.
  5. Measurement. Use Page Insights or Ads Manager for reach, CTR and paid results, and tagged links plus order data for conversion and CPA. Review weekly and adjust the weakest stage.
