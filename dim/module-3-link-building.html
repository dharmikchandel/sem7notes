3.1 / Link building

Backlinks, types and precursors

Link building is the process of earning links from other websites to your own. A credible, relevant backlink serves as a vote of confidence and can improve visibility, authority, referral traffic and brand awareness.

Start with the direction of the link

TypeHow it worksExample
InternalOne page links to another on the same site. Improves navigation, crawl discovery and distribution of link equity.A guide links to its product category.
External inboundAnother domain links to your page. An editorial backlink can signal relevance and credibility and send referral traffic.A college resource page cites your guide.
External outboundYour page links to another website, for instance as a supporting reference.A blog cites a government report.
Followed / nofollowA regular followed link can pass equity. A link marked rel="nofollow" can still send visitors.Editorial citation versus a link qualified by the publisher.
Natural / manualA publisher discovers and cites a resource independently, or you actively seek coverage through outreach, partnerships and promotion.An unsolicited citation versus pitching a relevant resource page.

Precursors before outreach

Use this preparation sequence to answer the 2024 precursor question. It organizes the setup and targeting points in the slides into an exam structure.

  1. Set the objective and baseline. Decide whether the priority is qualified referral traffic, ranking for a topic or brand authority. Record present rankings, organic traffic and referring domains.
  2. Prepare the destination. Fix broken pages and navigation, make the page usable on mobile, and make sure the link target is stable and useful.
  3. Create a linkable asset. Build an original guide, infographic, tool, research report or case study that gives another publisher a reason to cite it.
  4. Identify the audience and linking prospects. Find related resource pages, blogs, publishers or associations. Examine competitor backlinks for opportunities. Check relevance, editorial credibility and audience fit.
  5. Prepare outreach and measurement. Develop a personalized pitch, assign time and owners, and track new referring domains, relevance, referral traffic and ranking changes.
Exam distinction. Precursors are preparation steps. Types classify links or acquisition methods. Elements are the ingredients of an effective campaign.

3.1 / Link building

Content acquisition and link marketing

  1. Identify a linkable topic and format. Use guides, original reports, case studies, infographics, data visualizations or interactive tools. The skyscraper approach improves a proven resource before approaching sites that cited it.
  2. Create and optimize. Make the material accurate, readable and useful. Use relevant search terms and descriptive headings.
  3. Distribute. Pitch curated resource pages, contribute guest posts, replace dead links, offer expert commentary to journalists (HARO in the notes), syndicate appropriately, and use reputable niche directories. Where a copy is syndicated, the slides suggest a canonical reference to the original.
  4. Build relationships. Share on social, participate meaningfully in groups and forums, engage with influencers and follow up with tailored email that explains the value to their readers.
  5. Measure and refine. Track earned backlinks, quality, relevance, referral traffic and link diversity. Repeat the formats and pitches that work.

Link marketing is the broader promotion strategy encompassing link acquisition, relationships and distribution. Link building is the acquisition work within it. The outcomes are qualified referral visits, search visibility, and brand credibility. An occasional relevant reciprocal citation can make editorial sense. Systematic exchanges for ranking belong under link spam.

3.1 / Link building

Fight link spam

Link spam is the use of artificial links to manipulate search rankings. Examples include purchased ranking links, link farms, automated comments and excessive link exchanges. Consequences include ignored link value, lower visibility and manual action.

  1. Audit. Inspect the Links report in Search Console and use Ahrefs or Semrush for additional patterns.
  2. Verify. Check relevance, site quality, repeated exact-match anchor text and suspicious outbound link density. Treat a toxicity score as an investigation cue.
  3. Remove. Request removal of verified manipulative links, and correct link schemes you control. Qualify paid placements appropriately.
  4. Escalate selectively. The slides name Google’s Disavow Tool for serious unresolved unnatural link issues and Google’s reporting channel for link spam.
  5. Prevent and monitor. Review the profile regularly and earn editorial links with original content and relevant outreach.

The slides cite Penguin and the December 2022 link spam update as examples of search-engine responses.

3.2 / SEO analytics

Track activity and choose metrics

Start with the decision you need to make. Website activity tracking connects search exposure to arrivals, engagement and business outcomes. For each KPI, specify its definition, source, segment and comparison period.

QuestionMeasureRead it with
Can users find us?Search impressions, average position / keyword rankings, branded vs non-branded visibilitySearch Console and rank tracking, split by query and page.
Do searchers click?Organic CTR = search clicks ÷ search impressions × 100Compare by position, query, title and snippet.
Who arrives?Organic traffic, source / medium, device, landing pageDistinguish organic, social, referral and paid traffic.
Do users engage?Time on page / engagement time, session duration, bounce rateInterpret by intent. A quick visit can answer a simple query.
Is the page usable?Load time, mobile traffic and Core Web VitalsLCP measures loading, CLS layout stability, and FID is the teacher slide’s legacy responsiveness metric.
Who links to us?Backlink count, anchor text, relevance and distinct referring domainsSearch Console, Ahrefs or Semrush.
Does traffic deliver value?Conversions, conversion rate, revenue and average order valueCompare by channel, landing page, device and time period.

Tool pairing. Search Console covers search impressions, clicks, queries and position. Google Analytics covers onsite traffic, behavior and outcomes. Semrush, Ahrefs and SE Ranking support rank or backlink audits. Surfer SEO is a slide example for content audits.

3.2 / SEO analytics

Set up Google Analytics

  1. Define goals. Choose business actions such as a purchase, sign-up or qualified form submission.
  2. Collect. Create the analytics property and relevant web data stream. Install the tracking tag in the site head or via Google Tag Manager. Check that page views and test events arrive. Enable suitable enhanced measurement such as site search where applicable.
  3. Configure. Measure important actions as events and flag the key outcomes. Link Search Console to connect search visibility with landing-page behavior.
  4. Report. Review acquisition, landing pages, engagement and conversions by channel and device. Build custom reports for SEO and record changes or campaigns alongside trends.
  5. Act. Compare periods, inspect a funnel or a weak page, fix the likely cause and validate the effect.

Past-paper terminology. The older Audience report covers who visits (location, demographics, device, new or returning). Behavior covers what users do (pageviews, landing and exit pages, navigation flow, site search and speed). Users Flow shows paths from entry through subsequent interactions. In older UA terminology, a bounce was commonly explained as a single-page visit with no further interaction. In GA4 terminology, bounce rate is the share of sessions that were not engaged. Use the report terminology named in the question.

3.2 / SEO analytics

Interpret sales and diagnose conversion

Compare equivalent periods and use the same funnel and denominator. Segment by source, device and landing page: average figures can hide a mobile or campaign-specific issue. Separate acquisition (visits), conversion efficiency (rate) and value per order (AOV). Note changes to season, promotion, prices, inventory or tracking before inferring a cause.

Conversion rate = converting visitors ÷ visitors × 100. For a session-based question, use converting sessions ÷ sessions. AOV = revenue ÷ orders. For a simple one-purchase-per-visit example, revenue = visits × purchase rate (as a decimal) × AOV.

Organic periodVisitorsOrdersConversion rateRevenue at ₹500 AOV
Baseline1,000404%₹20,000
Current1,500453%₹22,500

Traffic rose 50% and revenue rose 12.5%, while conversion rate fell one percentage point. In analytics, compare mobile and desktop funnels. If the biggest change is the mobile checkout drop-off, test a shorter form and transparent delivery charges. Judge the change by completion rate and orders, with purchase-event tracking verified first.

  1. Landing page
  2. Product view
  3. Add to cart
  4. Checkout
  5. Purchase
  1. Define the conversion and benchmark the rate against your baseline, business target and a comparable period. Check data quality and volume.
  2. Map landing page, product view, cart, checkout and purchase. Split by channel, device and landing page to locate the biggest deterioration.
  3. Diagnose likely causes: irrelevant traffic, weak or duplicate content, confusing navigation, hidden CTA, slow or inconsistent mobile experience, complicated checkout, hidden pricing or missing reviews and trust signals.
  4. Choose a fix that matches the evidence. Examples include aligning page content with query intent, improving speed, clarifying a CTA, shortening the form or showing shipping early.
  5. A/B test a specific change and compare the target conversion rate and downstream sales against a baseline or control.

Legacy Analytics and natural link earning2021 objective and short-answer practice
  • Nov–Dec Q1A(4): A. Renaming the analytics account does not change the installed tracking identifier.
  • Nov–Dec Q1B(4). Behavior reports show what users do on the site, including visited pages, flows and exits.
  • Nov–Dec Q1B(6). Conversion rate is the percentage completing the intended action. Pay per action charges when a specified action, such as a purchase or lead, happens.
  • May–Jun Q1A(4): B. Users Flow visualizes visitor paths.
