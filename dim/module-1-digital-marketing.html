    module-1 › marketing-basics

    Digital vs traditional marketing

    Rank 1Asked 2022 and 202320 min
    The ideaTraditional marketing broadcasts one message to everyone and hopes the right people see it. Digital marketing picks the people, lets them answer back, and tells you exactly what happened. Every difference below is a version of that.
    CriterionDigital marketingTraditional marketing
    MediumInternet channels: search, social media, email, apps, websitesPrint, TV, radio, billboards, direct mail
    ReachGlobal, any connected user, any timeLocal or regional, limited to the medium's coverage
    CostLow entry cost, pay per click or impression, budget can scale up or downHigh fixed cost: print runs, airtime, hoardings
    TargetingPrecise by age, interest, location and behaviour; personalisedMass audience, little personalisation
    InteractionTwo-way: likes, comments, replies, instant purchaseMostly one-way; feedback is slow and indirect
    MeasurementReal-time analytics: clicks, conversions, ROIHard to measure; relies on surveys and sales estimates
    FlexibilityEdit, pause or optimise a live campaignFixed once printed or aired
    Availability24x7, fast resultsLimited slots, long lead time
    How to write it for marks (2022 Q1A)Draw the table with the criterion in a narrow first column, then one line per side. Choose the five you can explain fastest: reach, cost, targeting, interaction, measurement. If the examiner wants strictly two columns, write "Reach: ..." at the start of each cell. Finish with one line: the best campaigns blend both.

    Advantages of digital over traditional (2023 Q2A)

    Same table, flipped into positives. Write six to eight, each with a one-line reason:

    • Global reach: a small Mumbai brand can sell to London.
    • Cost-effective: small and medium businesses compete with large ones on a level field.
    • Measurable: analytics show what works, so money moves to winners.
    • Targeted and personalised: higher conversion and ROI because ads reach the interested.
    • Interactive: direct conversations build loyalty and give feedback.
    • Flexible and real-time: adjust a campaign mid-way.
    • Mobile access: reach people on the device they use most.
    • Easy to scale: turn a campaign up for a sale season, down afterwards.
    Memory hook for the first six: Global, Cost, Measurable, Targeted, Interactive, Flexible. "Great Cats Make Terrific Interactive Friends."
    Watch outDo not call traditional marketing useless. Examiners reward the balanced line that it still works for local, offline and older audiences.

    Check yourself

    Which five criteria would you pick for a two-column answer?
    Reach, cost, targeting, interaction, measurement. They are the easiest to explain with an example each.
    Why is digital marketing easier to optimise?
    Real-time analytics show results immediately, and a live campaign can be edited, paused or reallocated. A printed ad cannot.
    Give one case where traditional marketing still wins.
    A local shop reaching nearby residents with a hoarding or pamphlet, or a brand targeting people who are not online much.

    module-1 › channels › ppc

    PPC: stages and the four metrics

    Rank 1Asked 2022 (Q1B)35 min
    The ideaIn pay-per-click advertising you bid on a keyword and pay only when someone actually clicks. Showing the ad is free. Because every click costs money, the whole discipline is: get cheap clicks from people who then do something valuable.

    Definition. PPC is a model where the advertiser pays only when a user clicks the ad and visits the landing page, not for the placement itself. In search advertising, you choose a keyword and bid on it. When a user searches it, your ad appears at the top of the results.

    Benefits. Easy to track (impressions, clicks, conversions). Budget is under your control. It does not depend on algorithm changes, so it works even when organic ranking is weak.

    Platforms. Google Ads is the biggest (average CPC about $2 to $4 in your slides), Bing or Microsoft Ads (about $1.5, mainly PC users), Facebook Ads (about $0.95 per click, strong on mobile).

    The four stages

    Stage 1: GoalsChoose the business goal and its KPI. Understand ad rank and quality score.
    Stage 2: SetupCampaign type, location, language, device, budget, bidding, keywords, ad copy.
    Stage 3: ManageMonitor, tune keywords and bids, add negative keywords, A/B test ads.
    Stage 4: AnalyzeRead reports, find wasted spend, carry lessons into the next campaign.

    After Analyze you loop back to Goals with better information. That loop is why PPC is called a cycle.

    Open the detail for each stage

    Goals

    Match goal to KPI: traffic means clicks and sessions; brand awareness means impressions and reach; leads means number of leads; sales means revenue and conversions; engagement means time on site and page views; app promotion means downloads. Everything later (keywords, ads, bids, budget) depends on this choice.

    Setup

    Campaign types: Search (text ads in results), Display (banners on partner sites), Shopping (product ads with image and price), Video (YouTube), App (promote installs). Settings: name, geography, language, device. Bidding: manual CPC (you cap each keyword) or automatic (Google adjusts to hit your goal).

    Manage

    Keep high-performing keywords, drop weak ones, add new relevant ones, update match types, add negative keywords so you stop paying for irrelevant searches. Review bids as competition changes. Test headlines, descriptions, call to action and landing pages in A/B tests.

    Analyze

    Reports: campaign, ad group, keyword, search query, geographic, device, conversion. They answer: were the goals met, which ads and keywords convert, where is money wasted, what to change next time.

    The metrics (this is the 2022 Q1B answer)

    MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
    ImpressionsTimes the ad was shownVisibility, whether clicked or not
    CTR (click-through rate)Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100How relevant and engaging the ad is
    CPC (cost per click)Total cost ÷ Total clicksWhat each visit costs; lower means more clicks for the same budget
    Conversion rateConversions ÷ Clicks × 100Share of clickers who complete the goal (buy, register, download)
    CPA (cost per acquisition)Advertising cost ÷ ConversionsWhat one successful conversion costs
    ROI(Revenue − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100Whether the campaign is profitable
    Watch outCTR divides by impressions. Conversion rate divides by clicks (in PPC). Mixing those two denominators is the commonest lost mark. Your general measurement slide divides conversion rate by visitors, so say "visitors or clicks" if the question is not PPC specific.

    Metric lab: change the numbers and watch them move

    Before editing, predict: with the defaults, will the CPA be above or below ₹250? Then check.

    -CTR
    -CPC
    -Conversion rate
    -CPA
    -ROAS (revenue ÷ cost)
    -ROI

    Quality Score

    Google's rating of how useful your ad is. It depends on expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page quality. A higher score gives a better ad position, lower CPC and more visibility. That is why a relevant ad can beat a bigger bid.

    How to write it for marksFor "define the following terms": one line definition, the formula, and a tiny example for each. A worked example of the same ₹5,000 campaign across all four terms earns the full marks and shows you understand the link between them.

    Check yourself

    Name the four PPC stages in order.
    Goals, Setup, Manage, Analyze, then back to Goals.
    Which stage is "add negative keywords" in, and why?
    Manage. You learn from live search queries which irrelevant searches waste money, then block them.
    A campaign costs ₹6,000, gets 300 clicks and 15 conversions. CPC, conversion rate, CPA?
    CPC = 6,000 ÷ 300 = ₹20. Conversion rate = 15 ÷ 300 × 100 = 5%. CPA = 6,000 ÷ 15 = ₹400.
    Three things that make up Quality Score?
    Expected CTR, ad relevance, landing page quality.

    module-1 › capturing-customers › online-techniques

    Capturing customers with online marketing techniques

    Rank 1Asked 2025 (Q1, 10 marks)35 min
    The idea"Capturing customers" is a funnel: reach people, engage them, convert them, keep them. Each technique below does one or more of those jobs. If you build your 10-mark answer around the funnel, it reads as an argument and not a list.

    1. Web 2.0 interactive techniques

    Web 1.0 let people read. Web 2.0 lets them create, share and respond, which is what lets a business talk with customers and not at them.

    TechniqueWhat it isHow it captures customers
    Social media marketingFacebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, YouTube: posts, comments, shares, live sessionsAwareness, direct engagement, viral sharing, leads
    BloggingRegular articles with comments and sharingEducates, builds authority, brings organic traffic
    MicrobloggingShort updates on X or ThreadsInstant communication, trend participation, real-time support
    WikisCollaboratively edited knowledge pagesProduct documentation, self-service support
    Social bookmarkingUsers save and share pages (Pinterest, Flipboard)Content discovery, referral traffic, SEO visibility
    Reviews and ratingsGoogle Reviews, Yelp, Amazon reviewsSocial proof and trust, which nudges the buying decision
    Live streamingReal-time video: launches, Q&A, demosInstant interaction and feedback

    2. Video and vlogs

    People connect with faces and stories, not logos. Three reasons it works: vlogs feel authentic so trust rises; viewers retain far more from video than from text (your slide says roughly 95% against 10%); and platforms push video in feeds.

    • Behind-the-scenes vlogs: show the human side, goal is emotional connection.
    • Short-form videos (Reels, Shorts, TikTok, under 60 seconds): quick tips, rapid discovery and broad organic reach.
    • Product demos and problem-solvers: show the product solving a pain point, removes buying hesitation.
    • Customer stories and user-generated content: real testimonials give social proof.

    3. Webcasts, web conferences and webinars

    WebcastWebinarWeb conference
    DirectionMostly one-way broadcast to a large audienceTwo-way: presenter plus Q&A, polls, chatMany-to-many meeting in a small group
    Typical useLaunch, keynote, training broadcastLead generation, demos, workshopsClient meetings, collaboration with screen sharing
    Value to marketerReach and credibilityRegistration forms capture leads; interaction qualifies themClosing deals, onboarding

    How webcasts capture customers: they reach people anywhere, showcase expertise, engage through live Q&A, generate qualified leads through registration forms, demonstrate the product, and let you follow up with recordings and emails. Attendance and engagement data show what to improve.

    Not fully in your slidesYour slides cover webcasts in detail. The webinar vs web conference distinction above is the standard definition, safe to write in an exam.

    4. Podcasts

    Streamable or downloadable audio episodes. Uses: brand storytelling, product education, expert interviews. Strengths: builds credibility, reaches niche audiences, long-term loyalty because listeners subscribe.

    5. Mobile devices

    SMS, apps, mobile-friendly sites and location-based offers reach people on the device they carry all day. Full treatment in the mobile marketing topic below.

    How to write it for marks (2025 Q1)One line defining online marketing, then five or six techniques. For each: what it is, how it captures a customer, one example (Nike on Instagram, a clothing brand's try-on vlog, a bank's webinar). Draw a small funnel (reach, engage, convert, retain) and place each technique on it. Close with one line: the techniques work best combined and measured.

    Check yourself

    Four video formats that capture customers?
    Behind-the-scenes vlogs, short-form videos, product demos, customer stories and user-generated content.
    How is a webinar different from a webcast?
    A webinar is two-way (Q&A, polls, chat) and captures leads through registration. A webcast is mainly a one-way broadcast to a large audience.
    Why do reviews and ratings matter for capturing customers?
    Social proof. Buyers trust other customers more than the brand, so reviews lower hesitation and influence the purchase.

    module-1 › channels › mobile-marketing

    Mobile marketing in four stages

    Rank 1Asked 2025 (Q2)25 min
    The ideaMobile marketing promotes products through phones and tablets: SMS and MMS, apps, mobile sites, and location-aware offers. The edge over other channels is that the phone is personal, always on, and knows where the customer is.

    Types, grouped so you can remember them

    FamilyTypes
    MessagingDirect SMS or MMS marketing, AI-driven chatbots
    Apps and sitesResponsive mobile websites, interactive mobile apps, in-app advertising, mobile wallet marketing
    Social and videoSocial commerce, optimised social media marketing, engaging mobile video ads, in-game advertising
    Location-basedGPS or beacon targeting, Bluetooth proximity marketing, QR codes
    EmergingAugmented reality, voice search optimisation

    Why it matters: expanded reach, precise targeting, better user experience, geographically targeted outreach, agile adaptation. Brand examples from your slides: IKEA's interactive initiatives, Burger King's mobile outreach, Swiggy's mobile engagement.

    The process, and why the question says "four stages"

    Your slides list seven steps: understand the audience; choose a mobile approach; build an opt-in database; make the website mobile-friendly; use native ads; use QR codes; monitor with analytics. The exam asks for four stages, so group them:

    Stage 1: Know the audienceStep 1
    Stage 2: Choose approach and channelsSteps 2, 5 and 6 (strategy, native ads, QR codes)
    Stage 3: Build the base and permissionSteps 3 and 4 (opt-in list, mobile-friendly site)
    Stage 4: Measure and adaptStep 7 (analytics)
    My grouping, not your slides'Any sensible four-stage grouping of the seven steps is acceptable. What the examiner is checking is that each stage is applied to the gym.
    Try first (6 minutes): the 24-hour gym in Manhattan. Write four stages, then open this.

    Case: new gym, audience is working professionals aged 25 to 40 who live nearby or visit for business, corporate packages are a new venture.

    Stage 1: Know and segment the audience

    Three personas: local professionals who train very early or very late; business travellers staying nearby; HR or office managers who buy corporate packages. Their mobile habits: commute browsing, searches like "24 hour gym near me", LinkedIn during work hours, hotel stays.

    Stage 2: Choose approach and channels

    Local mobile search and a Google Business profile (voice search too). Geo-targeted social and video ads around offices and hotels. Location-based push or SMS offers when someone is a few blocks away. QR codes in hotel lobbies and office buildings. Native ads or LinkedIn ads for the corporate package.

    Stage 3: Build the base and permission

    A fast, responsive mobile site with one-tap trial booking and a corporate enquiry form. An opt-in SMS list (free day pass for signing up). A mobile wallet pass for entry and offers, and a chatbot for booking questions.

    Stage 4: Measure and adapt

    Track sign-ups, day-pass redemptions, CTR and cost per acquisition by channel. A/B test offers and send times. Send timely, personalised messages ("late-night class open now"). Scale what works, add referral rewards for corporate accounts.

    How to write it for marksName each stage, then spend two or three lines tying it to the case. A generic answer that never mentions the 25 to 40 professionals or corporate packages loses most of the marks.

    Check yourself

    Name your four stages.
    Know the audience, choose approach and channels, build the base and permission (opt-in, mobile site), measure and adapt.
    Why is an opt-in list essential for SMS?
    It complies with regulation and makes sure messages reach people who actually want them.
    Which mobile type uses GPS or beacons?
    Location-based marketing. Proximity marketing is the Bluetooth version.

    module-1 › channels › email-marketing

    Email marketing and its ten rules

    Rank 1Asked 2023 (Q3 option)25 min
    The ideaEmail marketing sends commercial emails to existing and potential customers to build relationships, promote offers and bring people back. It is cheap, direct and measurable, but only works if people want your emails.

    Types

    PromotionalNewsletterWelcomeCart abandonmentCustomer re-engagementOnboarding dripHoliday or eventRatings and reviews

    Two worth explaining in a line: a cart abandonment email reminds shoppers who left items in the cart to return and check out; a welcome email is the first contact after sign-up and sets the tone of the relationship.

    How a campaign works (six steps)

    Define audiencePersonas, demographics, interests
    Set goalsTraffic, leads, sales; KPIs like open and click rates
    Build the listOpt-in forms, events, partnerships; incentivise
    Pick campaign typePromo, newsletter, win-back, drip
    Make a scheduleCadence per segment, automation
    MeasureLink analytics and UTM tags; keep what works

    Benefits: high ROI, targeted reach, measurable results, cost-effective, direct line to customers.

    Ten rules for effective email marketing (2023 Q3)

    The question asks for ten unique rules. Use these, each with its reason:

    1. Get permission first. Use opt-in sign-ups, never bought lists. Consent is both law and good targeting.
    2. Segment your list. Different groups get different messages, so relevance rises.
    3. Personalise. Name, past purchases and behaviour make an email feel written for one person.
    4. Write a strong subject line and preview text. Short, specific, honest. It decides whether the email is opened.
    5. One clear call to action. Each email should push one next step (buy, register, read).
    6. Design for mobile. Most emails are opened on phones, so use a responsive layout and tappable buttons.
    7. Give value, not just offers. Tips, news and exclusives keep people subscribed.
    8. Choose timing and frequency carefully. Be consistent without causing fatigue.
    9. Include an unsubscribe link and sender identity. It is legally required and protects deliverability.
    10. Test and track. A/B test subject lines and CTAs; watch open rate, CTR, conversions and unsubscribes.

    Spare rules if you need a swap: keep the list clean by removing bounced and inactive addresses; keep a recognisable sender name and brand style.

    Not in your slidesYour slides cover types, working steps and benefits. The ten rules are standard best practice built on those, plus the email law points from the Law topic. Write them in your own words.

    Check yourself

    Which two rules are legal requirements?
    Prior consent (opt-in) and an unsubscribe link with clear sender identity.
    What is a drip campaign?
    A scheduled series of helpful emails, for example onboarding over the first 30, 60 or 90 days.
    What does an A/B test compare in email?
    Two versions of one element (subject line, CTA, send time) on parts of the list, to see which performs better.

    module-1 › strategy › measurement-roi

    Strategy, measurement and ROI formulas

    Rank 2Syllabus 1.1, not asked yet35 min
    The ideaA strategy says what you want and how you will get it. Measurement tells you if it worked. The formulas are the language of "worked", so they are the likeliest numerical question.

    Building a digital marketing strategy

    GoalsSMART: specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, timely
    Buyer personasFictional customers built from real data
    Audit assetsRank existing content and channels
    Plan resourcesBudget, people, tech, deadlines
    DistributeOwned, earned and paid channels
    Pick KPIsConversion rate, cost per lead, CTR, CLV

    Goal vs strategy vs objective: the goal is the outcome ("raise awareness of our widgets"), the strategy is how ("content marketing across the funnel"), the objective is the measurable check ("25% more whitepaper downloads each quarter").

    Owned = your website, blog, email, social pages. Earned = what others share or write about you. Paid = PPC and influencers.

    InboundContent marketingABMSEOSocial mediaEmailPPCVideoEventsChatbots and live chatEarned media

    The eleven strategies your notes list. Know the names; explain two or three.

    Planning, then monitoring. Plan: objectives, audience, channels, messaging, content, budget, timeline, tracking, team roles, launch. Monitor: watch real-time metrics, analyse data, assess ROI, read engagement and feedback, A/B test, adjust elements, reallocate budget, report, improve, adapt to outside changes.

    KPIs by area

    AreaExamples
    WebsiteTraffic, page views, bounce rate, average session duration
    SEOOrganic traffic, keyword ranking, backlinks
    Social mediaLikes, shares, comments, followers, reach
    EmailOpen rate, CTR, unsubscribe rate
    PPCCPC, conversion rate, CPA
    E-commerceSales, revenue, average order value

    Formulas with worked examples

    MetricFormulaExample
    Bounce rateSingle-page visits ÷ total visits × 100300 of 1,000 visits leave after one page: 30%
    CTRClicks ÷ impressions × 100500 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 5%
    Conversion rateConversions ÷ visitors × 10040 ÷ 800 × 100 = 5%
    CAC (customer acquisition cost)Total marketing cost ÷ new customers₹50,000 ÷ 100 = ₹500
    CLV (customer lifetime value)Average purchase value × purchase frequency × customer lifespan₹500 × 4 a year × 3 years = ₹6,000
    ROAS (return on ad spend)Revenue from ads ÷ advertising cost₹50,000 ÷ ₹10,000 = 5, so every ₹1 brought ₹5
    ROI(Revenue − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100(80,000 − 20,000) ÷ 20,000 × 100 = 300%
    Watch outROAS is a ratio of revenue to ad spend. ROI uses profit after cost and is a percentage. A campaign can have ROAS 2 and still lose money once other costs are counted.

    Check yourself

    Cost ₹30,000, revenue ₹90,000. ROI and ROAS?
    ROI = (90,000 − 30,000) ÷ 30,000 × 100 = 200%. ROAS = 90,000 ÷ 30,000 = 3.
    Spell out SMART.
    Specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, timely.
    Owned vs earned vs paid, with one example each?
    Owned: your blog. Earned: a news site writes about you. Paid: an influencer sponsorship or PPC ad.

    Rank 2Syllabus 1.3, not asked yet15 min
    The ideaMarketing runs on trust, and these laws protect the customer's data, wallet and attention. Compliance builds trust and avoids penalties. Each area boils down to: get consent, tell the truth, credit others, be transparent.
    AreaWhat it coversWhat a marketer must do
    Data privacyGDPR (consent for collecting and processing data), CCPA (consumer rights), national lawsExplicit consent, clear privacy policy, easy way to opt out
    Advertising standardsFTC truth-in-advertising, ASA guidelines on claims, promotions, influencersLabel sponsored posts, no false claims, genuine testimonials

    Email marketingRules on unsolicited emailPrior consent, unsubscribe link, identify the sender
    E-commerce and consumer protectionConsumer-rights lawsClear pricing and terms, honour return and refund rules, avoid dark patterns
    Social media and influencersPaid relationships must be visibleDisclose, monitor content, keep records of paid partnerships

    Memory sentence for the seven areas in order: "Dan Always Insists Everyone Eats Sweet Cookies." Data, Ads, IP, Email, E-commerce, Social, Cookies.

    Check yourself

    Which two laws overlap with email marketing rules?
    Data privacy (consent) and the email law itself (unsubscribe link, sender identity).
    What is a dark pattern?
    A design trick that pushes users into choices they would not otherwise make, such as hidden fees or a hard-to-find cancel button.

    module-1 › overview

    Skim: evolution, importance and the channel list

    Page 210 min, read once

    Meaning

    Digital marketing uses digital channels, platforms and technologies to promote products, services or brands to a target audience, to create awareness, generate leads, drive sales and build relationships. Google's definition: an umbrella term for all online marketing efforts (search, social, email, websites). Forrester's angle: using digital technology to analyse campaigns in real time.

    Evolution in one line each

    • 1990s: internet, basic websites, email, banner ads.
    • Late 1990s to early 2000s: SEO and PPC; Google AdWords launched in 2000.
    • Mid-2000s on: social media; user-generated content.
    • 2010s on: mobile marketing, content and inbound marketing, big data and analytics, video and live streaming, AI and chatbots, voice search, AR and VR, ephemeral content and influencer marketing.
